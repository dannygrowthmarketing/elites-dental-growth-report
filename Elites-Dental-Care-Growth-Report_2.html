01
Performance Overview

Executive Summary

4 August to 17 September. One campaign, ₹25,122, eight bookings. Now switched off.

Click-through was 6.86%, cost per click normal. Nothing before the click is broken. Everything after it is.

Six of the eight bookings landed on two days in August. Forty-five of forty-nine days produced nothing.

The account is not short of budget. It is short of direction.

Main findings — what is working

  • 4.9 from 271 reviews, every one answered personally by the owner.
  • 6.86% click-through — above benchmark for local dental.
  • Sunday mornings returned ₹421 per booking. Five of the eight.
  • The ad account is already structured by treatment — nine themed groups exist.
  • Treatment pages are substantial: 1,300–1,700 words, real FAQs, six MDS specialists named.
  • Conversion tracking verifies the booking API before firing.
  • Google Posts weekly, 78+ photos, profile actively maintained.

Main findings — what needs attention

  • ₹22,379 of ₹25,122 — 89% — on days that produced nothing.
  • 76% of budget on generic “near me”. Implants, braces and aligners got 3.6%.
  • Dental Implants ad group: 75 impressions, one click, seven weeks. A catch-all named “Original” took 67% of impressions.
  • 251 price-intent searches reached the ads and converted nothing. No page answers what anything costs.
  • Zero page-one presence for “dental clinic tc palya” or “dentist in tc palya”. Not on Practo, Lybrate or Bajaj Health; the Justdial listing sits under a third locality name.
  • No H1, no meta description, no Dentist schema on any commercial page.
  • 41 junk URLs indexed — 13 Latin placeholders from 2015.
  • Conversion value hardcoded to ₹1. Bidding cannot tell an implant enquiry from a scaling appointment.
  • No Meta Pixel, no tag manager. 448 paid visitors, none retargetable.
Revenue Opportunity

Same budget, better direction

Every rupee was spent at a normal cost per click. The loss was direction — cheapest-intent searches, worst-converting hours, slowest page.

The same ₹50,000 at a 4% booking rate — a third of what Sunday mornings already hit — takes cost per booking from ₹3,140 to ₹1,339. Model it in section 10.

Reputation Opportunity

271 reviews, two followers

271 patients wrote a public review. Two follow Facebook. Thirty follow Instagram.

The clinic has already earned advocacy — the hardest thing in local marketing — and asks nothing with it. Closing that costs nothing.

Account Structure

The right structure, starved

The account is already built by treatment. Nine ad groups — Dental Implants, Orthodontics, Root Canal, Crowns, Dentures, Whitening, Paediatrics, General Dentist, and a catch-all called “Original”. The structural work is done.

Then the catch-all ate it. “Original” took 67% of all impressions. The six treatment groups together got 338 impressions, 15 clicks, ₹798 in seven weeks. Dental Implants: 75 impressions, one click. Dentures: two impressions.

“Original” holds broad keywords that match nearly every dental query, so it wins the internal auction first. The fix is not restructuring. It is starving the catch-all and funding what already exists.

Two more. Close variants took 29% of impressions and ₹8,009 — the source of “bengali dentist near me” and “veeners near me”. And AI Max is switched on, delivering 22 impressions with no configuration behind it.

Delivery was never stable. Nine days had no impressions; 10–15 September was a six-day blackout. Then 16–17 September spent triple the daily average at ₹92 and ₹69 per click. The campaign never finished learning.

Post-Click Experience

Conversion Rate Optimization

96% of spend arrived on mobile and click-through was already healthy. The landing experience is the highest-leverage variable in the account. 469 qualified visits, 8 conversions — decided entirely after the click.

The first 3.3 seconds are empty. Server response alone takes 2,610 ms. Someone in discomfort does not wait.

Then the wrong thing appears. No headline, no phone number, no clinic name above the fold — a rotating carousel of service names over a stock photo. The positioning line sits on a slide most visitors never see.

Then four steps to book. Clinic, doctor, date, contact details — and step one offers a choice between one clinic. The widget is client-rendered, so search engines see an empty page.

Booking flow — current versus recommended

4Enter contact detailsMove to step 1Name and number first. Everything else can be settled on the phone.
Sequence matters. Fixing the 2.6-second response and the booking flow costs days and no media budget, and multiplies the return on every rupee after it. Restarting before that spends at the same 1.71% rate that produced this report.

Method and limits. Google Ads figures come from account 520-119-6364, exported 18 September 2026 — campaign, ad schedule, search keyword, search terms (1,029 rows), device, demographic, reach and frequency, and daily time-series reports covering 1 August to 18 September. Site structure, headings, meta tags, schema, alt coverage, page weight and load timings were measured on the live site the same day. Search results were checked on Google India, signed out.   • No GA4 or Search Console access; organic statements are external observations.
  • Social profiles are behind login walls beyond the first screen, so post counts and engagement history are unverified.
  • The Business Profile was read signed out, so an unpublished services list would look identical to an empty one.
Nothing here is estimated and presented as measured. Where a number is an assumption, it says so on the same line.
